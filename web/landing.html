Nepal can't predict when the mountain falls. It can predict who has time to run.

On the night of Oct 11, 2026, a flash flood came down the Budhi Gandaki in Gorkha. A villagers' phone relay reached Dharche at 03:30. The official SMS went out at 06:40, after the first wave had hit Arughat. Time to Safety turns a trigger upstream into the minutes each village has, and the nearest ground the water won't reach.

How it works

The flood's arrival time and height come from the real Oct 11 and Aug 26 floods, kilometre by kilometre down the river. A model trained on satellite flood maps scores every 30 m of ground. A terrain-aware walk finds who can reach safe ground before the wave.

Why the z17

A flood's height is never certain. We score every spot under 100 possible floods, about 10 million predictions, on the Telum on-chip AI accelerator in under 3 seconds, 31× faster than the processor in our benchmark. It runs where the data lives, with nothing sent off the machine.

Data: Copernicus GLO-30 DEM, © OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL), WorldPop (CC BY 4.0), UNOSAT flood maps. Built with GRASS GIS, scikit-learn, IBM zDNN, FastAPI and Leaflet. Source and method on GitHub.
